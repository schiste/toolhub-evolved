# Technical and Marketing Notes

- Ranking is plain SQL over two `LIKE` shapes, portable to the SQLite the tests run on and the MariaDB production runs on. The indexed text and the query are tokenized the same way: casefold, split on anything that is not a letter or digit, pad with one space each side. A whole word is then `LIKE '% term %'` and a word prefix is `LIKE '% term%'`, with no regular expression, full-text index or dialect branch. "x-tools", "x tools" and "x_tools" are the same two words on both sides of the comparison.
- The score is coverage, then placement, then exactness. Each query term the row contains at all is worth 1000; each term's best placement adds 60 for a whole word of the title, 40 for a title prefix, 35 and 25 for the keywords, 20, 12 and 5 for a word, prefix or bare substring anywhere; a title or name that is the entire query adds 500, more than the eight-term placement ceiling of 480. A row containing every word therefore always outranks a row containing fewer, and "XTools" always precedes "XTools ArticleInfo". Ties break on title length, the length norm a search engine would apply, then on name.
- `search_text` now covers name, title, subtitle, keywords and description, with the title and keywords also in two new columns, `search_title` and `search_keywords`, so they can be weighed apart. The existing deploy backfill walks a NULL `search_title` as its cursor and re-derives the whole catalog in batches of 500. In the window before it finishes, a row in the older newline-joined form is still found by the substring test; it only ranks by its text rather than its title.
- Any term can match now, replacing the AND that was the honest choice while nothing ranked. Terms are matched as words or word prefixes of a plain plural stem, so "ores" reaches "ore" and "ores" but "stores" only as a bottom-tier substring. The MCP `search_tools` description was rewritten: it claimed keywords were searched when they were not, and it now states the ranking contract instead of "every word must appear".
- Measured before shipping on a synthetic 25,000-row catalog through `search_payload`: 50 to 180 ms per page in the worst case where every row matches a term, 5 ms without a query. Production MariaDB timings are unmeasured until deploy; the check is Server-Timing on `/v1/catalog/search/tools/`, not a laptop number.
- Validation: 3,755 proxy tests pass, with eight new ones in `test_catalog_read.py` naming the production failures they fix (exact title first, whole word over substring, keywords and plurals, partial matches last, hyphenated names, explicit ordering kept, un-backfilled rows still found) and four rewritten from the AND contract. Broker gates for ruff, cspell, prettier, jscpd and pytest all passed at promotion.
