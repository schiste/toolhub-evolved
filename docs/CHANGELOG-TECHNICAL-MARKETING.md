# Technical and Marketing Notes

- `job_guard.sh` derived its reclaim window from each job's timeout — twice it, on the reasoning that nothing alive can hold a lock past twice the point the platform kills it. That is sound for "could this still be running" and wrong for "how long is the job silent afterwards": the threshold has to be crossed _and then noticed by a run_, so any job scheduled tighter than its own threshold loses every tick in between.
- Measured across jobs.yaml on 2026-09-04, one abandoned lock cost 7 of 32 scheduled jobs at least one run: 1 for `inference-enrichment`, 2 for `digest-deliver`, 10 for `people-reconcile-incremental`, and 60 for `api-cache-invalidator` — a minute schedule against the 3600s default, an hour of silence from one killed pod. The worst case is now 5 and four of the seven drop to 0.
- The owner heartbeats its lock every 30s, so `lock_age_seconds` — which already read the directory's mtime — now means "silent since" rather than "started at" with no change to the reading logic. Liveness is measured rather than inferred, which is what lets one 330s window be correct for a 120s job and a 2700s one alike.
- The heartbeat uses `touch -c`. A plain `touch` on a lock reclaimed underneath it would recreate the path as a _file_, which `mkdir` can never take again — the job would skip forever, locked out permanently by the thing meant to keep it running. A test removes the lock under a live heartbeat and asserts the path is not left as a file.
- It also runs detached from the job's stdout and stderr. Killing the subshell does not kill the `sleep` it is blocked in, and an orphaned sleep holding the output pipe makes every caller that reads to EOF wait out the full interval after the run is over. That surfaced as the guard's own test suite hanging for 30s per invocation.
- 330 rather than a round 300 because a threshold is only ever compared against by a run, so one landing on a schedule is decided by jitter — 300 sits exactly on `digest-deliver`'s five minutes. That is the failure `test_no_reclaim_threshold_lands_on_the_schedule_it_will_be_measured_against` already documented for `people-identity-reconcile`, whose 24 recorded reclaims ranged 3588-3613s against a 3600s threshold. The test now grades five-minute schedules too, since one number has to clear every period at once.
- The two invariants that encoded the old derivation are replaced rather than deleted: one asserts no job derives a window from its own timeout again, the other that the default leaves room for several missed heartbeats — the locks live on NFS, where an attribute cache can hide a fresh mtime for tens of seconds.
- Validation: 26 guard tests (24 existing plus a live run keeping its lock fresh and the file-creation guard), 36 worker tests, the full proxy suite, `ruff check`/`format`, prettier and cspell clean. `jobs.yaml` loses 25 `--stale-after` overrides and is unchanged otherwise; all 31 guarded commands still parse. Three comments beside those jobs still asserted the derived rule as fact -- that every guarded job sets the window to twice its own timeout -- and were corrected in the same release rather than left to mislead the next reader; the hazard one of them recorded, a threshold landing on the schedule it is measured against, is kept because it still applies to the single window.
