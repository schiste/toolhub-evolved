# Technical and Marketing Notes

- The width of a locking job was derived from the source twice and wrong both times. Two came from `advisory_lock` holding a connection while a session works underneath it; three from `run_job` taking an _intent_ lock alongside the real one when a caller queues. `people-identity-reconcile` kept timing out on three, because `people_reconcile.run()` takes a third advisory lock -- source attestations' writer lock -- fourteen stack frames below the entrypoint. Reading `run_job` answers what the scaffold holds; the question was what the _process_ holds, and those differ by any lock a body takes on its own.
- Instrumenting settled it in one run. A listener on pool checkout and return, with the ceiling lifted recorded peak concurrency for every lock-taking entrypoint against production on 2026-09-03: `people-identity-reconcile` 4, `source-attestations` 3, `projection-refresh` 3, `catalog-sync` 2, `statistics-refresh` 2, `catalog-projection` 2. `CONNECTIONS_PER_LOCKING_UNIT` is four, the worst observed. `projection-refresh` returning 3 rather than 5 is what kept this a constant change: at five nothing would have fitted and the answer would have been the ToolsDB grant.
- Four is the worst _observed_, not a proven maximum, and the source says so where the number is set. Every probe took its locks uncontested, and contention is when the extra connection is held longest. It also spends the last of the grant: 8 for the webservice fleet, 4 for the widest job, 6 for three others is 18, and 18 plus the reserved headroom is exactly the 20 available. A fifth does not fit, so the next body that nests another lock is a question about the grant rather than about this constant.
- `catalog_sync`, `account_sync` and `toolforge_account_sync` take advisory locks directly rather than through `run_job`, so the declaration added in the previous release never reached them and they kept a two-connection pool; `catalog-sync` timed out on it at 00:33:58. All three now declare it, and `toolforge_account_sync` passes it to `db.configure` itself because it does not go through `job_runner` at all. A test sweeps the entrypoints for `db.advisory_lock` and fails on any that does not declare -- checked against a deliberately reverted declaration, which it caught by name.
- Honest limits of that test: it reads entrypoints, so it cannot see a lock nested inside a body, which is exactly the case that caused this. It prevents the omission that hit three jobs, not the one that hit `people-identity-reconcile`. Measurement is what covers the second, and the measurement is a snapshot rather than a guarantee.
- Validation: 3,607 proxy tests pass with 25 skipped, `ruff check`, `ruff format` and cspell clean, graph index regenerated with the pinned 0.4.2 pair. The webservice half of this budget remains untouched by any of it and has served since its own fix without a single connection failure. Probe jobs and files were removed from the tool account afterwards.
