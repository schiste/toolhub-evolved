# Technical and Marketing Notes

- Both wiki lanes iterated a three-entry `GADGET_WIKIS` / `USERSCRIPT_WIKIS` list in `jobs.yaml`. They now take their wikis from a queue over the full readable set. Scope is `meta_p.wiki` in its entirety -- 1,028 rows, open and closed alike -- because that view already omits the wikis with no public replica. `is_sensitive` is explicitly _not_ an exclusion criterion: 203 rows carry it, `afwiktionary` among them, and it is publicly readable; treating the flag as "private" would have silently dropped a fifth of the projects.
- Per-wiki connections do not scale to a thousand and the Wiki Replicas say so. Wikis are now addressed by replica section -- `s1.labsdb`…`s8.labsdb` from `meta_p.wiki.slice` -- and one connection per section serves every wiki on it via `select_db`, so a full pass opens eight connections rather than 1,028. That is the documented remedy for "heavily crosswiki tools", and the connection is used for one pass and closed, never held idle. The queue orders its selection by section so a section's wikis are covered together.
- `wiki_projects` is a local roster refreshed weekly from `meta_p.wiki` (`wiki-registry`, Mondays 02:17), carrying dbname, section, family, language and closed state. An empty read is treated as a failed read and retires nothing -- the alternative is one bad `meta_p` query retiring the entire registry -- and wikis that disappear are stamped `retired_at` rather than deleted.
- `wiki_lane_state` is the queue: one row per wiki per lane, ordered by `next_due_at` with a wiki that has never run going first. Cadence is self-tuning -- halved when a pass changed something, doubled when it did not -- inside per-lane bounds: user scripts between one hour and thirty days, gadgets between six hours and fourteen, and closed wikis at ninety. Failures back off exponentially from 30 minutes to a week and keep the wiki's place in the rotation.
- Runs are bounded by a deadline, not a wiki count: `USERSCRIPT_BUDGET_SECONDS=1200` of the hourly tick and `GADGET_BUDGET_SECONDS=1800` of a now-six-hourly one. A deadline self-tunes to whatever capacity the replicas and the two dozen other jobs leave, and can never overrun the next tick the way a count can. It is checked between wikis and never during one -- a half-swept corpus is worse than a run that finished late -- so overrun is bounded by the cost of the last wiki started.
- A deliberate contract change: a failing wiki no longer fails the run. `job_guard.sh` disables a job after three consecutive failures, so under the old contract one wiki's outage would have disabled the census for the other 1,027 within three ticks. Failures are recorded on the wiki -- `consecutive_failures`, `last_error`, backoff -- and the run fails only when every wiki it attempted failed, which is the shape of a real problem: no credentials, no network, a broken deployment.
- `jobs.yaml` drops both wiki lists and carries the budgets instead; the gadget census moves from daily to every six hours, since the unit of work is now a slice of a queue rather than a fixed list. `GADGET_WIKIS` and `USERSCRIPT_WIKIS` survive as an operator override that outranks the queue -- still how one large corpus is swept on demand with `USERSCRIPT_SWEEP=1` -- and an empty queue over an empty registry falls back to the three original wikis, so a deployment has no census-shaped gap before the first registry refresh.
- Verification: 4 new suites (`wiki_registry`, its job, `wiki_schedule`, extended `wiki_replica`) and 3 extended ones, 238 tests in the affected set, covering the section-collapse arithmetic, the empty-roster guard, cadence tuning in both directions, budget exhaustion between wikis, per-wiki failure isolation with the backoff it records, the registry-empty fallback, and the fails-only-when-everything-failed contract.
